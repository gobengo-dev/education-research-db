# Sources Beyond OpenAlex for Australasian Educational Research Infrastructure

This report is written for a production metadata pipeline context rather than a library-guide context. The guiding question is not “what is educational research?” but “which sources will materially improve recall, local distinctiveness, author and institution linkage, and educational-relevance scoring in an AU/NZ-focused research graph?”. Where I describe likely OpenAlex or Crossref underrepresentation, that is an ingestion-risk estimate based on platform architecture, DOI visibility, issue structure, and local indexing patterns rather than a source-by-source OpenAlex API census.

## Australasian education journal landscape

Two broad patterns stand out. First, the strongest AU/NZ education journals are already fairly visible globally when they are on Springer, Sage, Taylor & Francis, Cambridge, or modern OJS installations with stable DOI practice. Second, a large tail of uniquely Australasian value sits in professional-association journals, local OJS titles, institutional platforms, conference archives, and long PDF backfiles where DOI practice is weak, title changes are common, and coverage leans on Informit/AEI, ERIC, or association websites rather than global DOI-first discovery. citeturn16search1turn19search6turn34search0turn34search1

It is the **local indexing layer** around Australian and New Zealand education: ACER’s AEI/A+ Education and specialist databases, Informit, NZCER-controlled titles, national library catalogues and archives, government evidence portals, and university-hosted journal and repository systems. These sources are especially important for **works without DOIs**, conference papers, reports, theses, and sector/professional literature. citeturn34search0turn34search1turn41search8turn41search14

## Technical harvesting recommendations

The single most useful technical distinction is by **platform archetype**, not by publisher name.

**OJS-based journals and proceedings** are the best immediate targets. PKP’s OJS explicitly supports discovery via Crossref, DOAJ and **OAI-PMH metadata harvesters**; PKP documentation also notes RSS/Atom support through plugins. In practice, many AU/NZ sources in this report sit on OJS or OJS-like publishing stacks: **AJET, MTED, Student Success, JTLGE, TESOL in Context, AIJRE, WJE, NZJTW, JOFDL, ASCILITE publications**, and several smaller titles. That means article URLs, issue archives, and machine-readable metadata are usually good enough for robust Python harvesters with low maintenance burden. citeturn19search6turn19search1turn18search1turn16search1turn21search1turn23search2turn23search3turn31search0turn24search3turn31search17turn31search6turn31search3turn38search0

**Association PDF archives** are the next most valuable class. **AARE**, **MERGA**, historical **ATEA** papers, and many practitioner journals expose large numbers of stable PDFs but weaker structured metadata. Here the right approach is not “scrape everything as HTML”; it is a two-stage pipeline: first harvest list pages and stable PDF URLs, then derive article-level metadata from page context, file naming, and PDF front matter. Maintenance burden is moderate, but the recall gain is large. citeturn37search0turn37search1turn36search5turn40search15turn30search2turn25search3

**Commercial indexing layers** such as **AEI, A+ Education and Informit** should be treated as partnership or licensed metadata sources, not scraping targets. They are too valuable to ignore and too awkward, legally and practically, to approach by brittle web harvesting. If you can secure access, they will likely add the highest number of missing Australian educational works, especially practitioner journals, reports, conference papers and theses. citeturn34search0turn34search1

**Government evidence portals** such as **AERO, CESE, ACARA, Education Counts, ERO and NZQA** are comparatively easy to crawl because they usually offer stable publication landing pages, obvious PDF links, and clear organisational provenance. The main challenge is not technical access; it is **metadata normalisation** and work typing. Many files need to be modelled as reports, briefs, evaluations, guides, or technical papers rather than journal articles. citeturn10search0turn7search4turn42search15turn4search1turn4search5turn42search6

**Institutional repositories** should be harvested selectively, not indiscriminately. Ingestion should start with repositories or journal platforms already known to host education outputs surfaced in this pass: **ECU**, **Deakin**, **AUT**, **Waikato**, **Massey**, **Canterbury**, and **Victoria University of Wellington**. The key is to target education collections, journal communities, school/faculty pages, and known researcher names rather than attempt whole-repository crawling from day one. citeturn20search4turn23search3turn31search6turn31search17turn25search4turn31search26turn32search12

The highest-value “low hanging fruit” from a technical perspective are:

- **OJS journal/proceedings clusters** with open archives and clear metadata;
- **AARE conference papers**;
- **NZCER journal family**;
- **AERO/CESE/ACARA/Education Counts/ERO/NZQA report libraries**; and
- **association practitioner journals** where issue archives are visible even if DOIs are weak. citeturn38search0turn37search0turn11search5turn10search0turn7search4turn42search15turn4search1turn4search5turn42search6turn29search3turn29search6turn32search3

The sources most likely to improve **author identity resolution** are **AARE papers**, **ATEA papers**, **NZARE abstracts/programmes**, **NZCER journals**, **Teacher magazine**, and institutional-journal platforms, because they frequently carry full author names, affiliations, and editorial or conference-role context. The sources most likely to improve **institution resolution** are government and think-tank report libraries, AARE/NZARE conference metadata, and university-hosted journals. The sources most likely to improve **educational relevance scoring** are the practitioner/professional sources and government evidence portals that sit closest to educational practice. citeturn37search0turn40search15turn39search4turn11search13turn29search0turn10search0turn7search4
